You've done this: gone back to a tweet you bookmarked and found it deleted, the
account gone, or the thread cut off right before the part you wanted. A bookmark is
just a pointer. When the post disappears, the pointer points at nothing, and
whatever you were keeping is gone with it.

FoundKeep saves the **content**, not the link. What you kept stays kept.

## What actually gets preserved

When you save a tweet or a thread, FoundKeep keeps a copy **server-side**:

- the **full text** — every post in the thread, and the whole of long-form
  **note-tweets**, the ones other tools only show a preview of and truncate,
- the **media** — the images and video attached to the posts.

So the save is still readable later even if the original is edited, protected, or
taken down. It's the difference between remembering that a thread existed and
actually having it.

## Then it files itself

Preservation is the foundation; the rest is what makes it usable. Each saved
tweet can be **summarized, tagged, and categorized** during processing, so it
doesn't just sit in a pile — it lands somewhere. Full-text
[search](/search-and-filters/) covers the preserved text, which means a phrase from
deep in a thread is enough to find it again. Filter by **type: tweet** to browse
everything you've kept from X in one place, add **personal tags** for the threads
worth returning to, and [link](/link-saves-and-graph/) a thread to your own notes
about it.

This post is the short version. The full walkthrough — how to save from desktop
and phone, and how to find threads later — lives in
[Keep tweets forever](/usecase-keep-tweets-forever/).

## How to do this in FoundKeep

1. Open the tweet or thread. On desktop, save it with the
   [browser extension](/save-from-anywhere/); on your phone, tap **Share** and
   choose **FoundKeep**.
2. FoundKeep pulls in the full text and media and stores it as a **tweet** —
   preserved server-side, not just a link.
3. Turn on **AI processing** so it gets a summary, tags, and a category.
4. Find it again with [search](/search-and-filters/), or filter by **type: tweet**.
5. Read the full guide in [Keep tweets forever](/usecase-keep-tweets-forever/).

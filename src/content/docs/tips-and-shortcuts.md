Once FoundKeep is part of your day, a few habits make it much more powerful.
Here are the ones worth learning.

## Re-run AI processing on an item

[AI processing](/privacy/) runs when you save something, but you can run it again
on any single item — useful if you saved before turning processing on, or if a
save came in before its content was fully available.

1. Open the save from your [dashboard](https://foundkeep.app/dashboard).
2. Re-run processing on it.
3. A fresh summary, tags, category, and OCR/text extraction replace the old ones.

## Lean on personal tags

AI tags give you coverage; **your** tags give you a vocabulary. A handful of
consistent personal tags — `read-later`, `reference`, `client-x`, `idea` — will do
more for findability than any amount of foldering. See [Folders & tags](/folders-and-tags/).

## Save the highlight, not just the page

When one passage is the reason you're saving, highlight it and save the
**selection**. You keep the exact quote, in context, without wading back through
the whole article later. See [Save from anywhere](/save-from-anywhere/).

## Link as you go

The moment you notice two saves belong together, [link them](/link-saves-and-graph/).
It costs a second now and saves a search later — and it's what grows your graph of
connections.

## Stack filters with search

Filters and search are stronger together. Filter to **type: image** and search a
word to find a screenshot by the text inside it; filter to **type: tweet** to
browse everything you've [preserved from X](/usecase-keep-tweets-forever/). See
[Search & filters](/search-and-filters/).

## Save from every device

Put FoundKeep on your phone's Share sheet *and* pin the browser extension. The
habit only sticks if saving is frictionless wherever you happen to be.

## Let your agent do the heavy lifting

If you use Claude or Codex, [connect them](/connect-claude-codex/) and ask them to
search, summarize, and tidy your library for you. See
[A second brain for agents](/usecase-second-brain-for-agents/).

Folders and tags group things. **Links** connect them. When two saves belong
together — a paper and the tweet that led you to it, a design and the brief it
answers — you can link them directly, and FoundKeep draws the result as a graph.

## Linking related saves

1. Open a save from your [dashboard](https://foundkeep.app/dashboard).
2. Choose to **link** it to another save.
3. Pick the related item. The link is mutual — it shows on both saves.

Link as many as make sense. A single idea might connect a note, an article, and a
screenshot; over time those links become the structure of what you're thinking
about.

## The graph of connections

Every link you make adds an edge to your **graph** — a map of how your saves
relate. Instead of a flat list, you get a picture:

- Clusters form around topics you keep returning to.
- A save that links to many others is usually a hub worth revisiting.
- Following edges surfaces things you'd forgotten you kept.

The graph is the difference between a folder of clippings and a web of ideas. It's
especially useful for research — see [A research library](/usecase-research-library/)
— and for building a [second brain your agent can use](/usecase-second-brain-for-agents/).

## Related saves

You don't have to link everything by hand. FoundKeep can also surface **related
saves** — items that look connected based on their content — so you can link the
good matches with a click and skip the rest.
